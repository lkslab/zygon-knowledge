Single-node capacity expansion under a CO2 cap

The optimiser co-decides capacities and hourly dispatch to minimise annualised capital plus operating cost. Tightening the CO2 cap moves the mix from gas to wind and solar, then to storage and hydrogen for the last few percent, and raises the CO2 shadow price.

Optimal capacity by CO2 cap
Power capacities per cap (electrolyser and fuel cell in MW electric). Gas gives way to wind and solar, and hydrogen appears only for the last few percent.
The last tonnes are the dearest
The same system re-planned at ever tighter CO2 caps. Halving emissions raises the average cost of power by a fifth; the last few percent cost more than everything before them. The CO2 price is what one more tonne allowed would save: it climbs from a few hundred to thousands of EUR per tonne as the cheap options run out.
Annual system cost by technology
Annualised capital plus operating cost. The gas bar shrinks and the wind, solar and storage bars grow; the total rises slowly until the cap approaches zero.
Price duration curves
Prices sorted from high to low over the share of hours (axis clipped at 400 EUR/MWh; scarcity hours reach the value of lost load). More zero-price hours and a few very high ones as the cap tightens: that is how wind and solar recover their capital cost.
