Carbon management: capture, sequestration and synthetic fuel

Electricity comes from wind, solar, gas and biomass. Gas plants emit to an atmosphere bus; carbon capture on gas and on biomass, and direct air capture, move CO2 to a captured bus, from where it is either sequestered or combined with electrolytic hydrogen into methanol for a fuel demand. A Store on the atmosphere bus holds the cumulative balance, and its end-of-period ceiling is the net-zero constraint; its bus price is the CO2 shadow price.

Annual CO2 flows (kt)
Carbon leaving the gas plants goes to the atmosphere or, through capture, to the captured pool; BECCS and DAC pull from the atmosphere into the same pool. From there it is buried or turned into methanol. The atmosphere's inflow minus its outflow is the net target.
Optimal capacity
Plants in MW of fuel input, DAC and the electrolyser in MW electric, methanolisation in MW hydrogen. What the optimiser builds to hit net zero at least cost.
Cumulative carbon balance
The atmosphere store rises when gas runs and falls when BECCS and DAC run; it must end at the target. The captured pool buffers between capture and use.
