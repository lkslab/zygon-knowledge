Backpressure and extraction CHP against a boiler and a heat pump

A backpressure unit has a fixed heat-to-power ratio: one Link with two outputs. An extraction-condensing unit can trade power for heat inside a feasible region bounded by the iso-fuel line (every unit of heat costs c_v units of power) and the backpressure line (power at least c_m times heat); that region is two custom linopy constraints on two Links sharing one fuel budget.

Feasible region of the extraction CHP
Power against heat: the iso-fuel line is full fuel, giving up c_v MW of power per MW of heat; the backpressure line is the most heat the turbine can extract. Operating points cluster on the backpressure line in cold hours and slide up the iso-fuel line when power is dear.
Heat supply over the fortnight
The backpressure unit runs flat, the extraction unit follows the heat load, the boiler and the heat pump take the peaks; which one depends on the electricity price of the hour.
Extraction CHP: power and heat against the electricity price
When the price rises the unit condenses more (power up, heat down) and the boiler covers the heat; when wind pushes the price down it extracts more heat.
