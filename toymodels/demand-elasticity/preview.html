Price-responsive demand and market clearing

Three clearings of the same fleet and day: a fixed load, a demand made of bid blocks (an inelastic core at the value of lost load plus tranches with falling willingness to pay) and a linear demand curve approximated by five equal blocks. Elastic demand shaves the peak, caps the price and makes welfare, not cost, the objective.

Where supply meets demand, and who gains
The plants' staircase against what consumers bid at the evening peak (the core's 3000 EUR/MWh runs off the top). They cross at 80 EUR/MWh and 9,000 MW: the last bids drop out before the peaker is needed. Blue is what consumers value above the price, green what plants earn above their cost.
Supply and demand curves at the peak hour
The supply staircase against the two demand staircases (the inelastic core at the value of lost load is clipped at 400). The price is where demand crosses supply; the fixed load is a vertical line at the right edge of the core.
Hourly price by demand case
With elastic demand the evening peak no longer reaches the peaker: the last tranche of demand drops out before the price climbs.
Welfare by demand case
Consumer surplus is willingness to pay minus price over what is consumed, producer surplus is price minus marginal cost over what is generated. The fixed load values every MWh at the value of lost load, hence its large consumer surplus.
