Electric vehicles: uncontrolled, smart and vehicle-to-grid charging

The fleet is one big battery that is only plugged in at night and around midday and must deliver a fixed driving demand in the morning and evening commutes. Uncontrolled charging takes power the moment the cars plug in; smart charging waits for the cheap hours; vehicle-to-grid also sells back at the evening peak. Same week, same cars, same prices to start from.

Same cars, same energy: when they charge is what matters
Two weekdays of demand. 100,000 cars plug in after the evening commute; charged at once (orange) they add about 560 MW on top of the evening peak. Charged when power is cheap (dashed green), the same energy goes into the cheap hours, at night and around midday, and the peak does not grow at all.
Charging power against the price
Grid draw of the fleet per case (vehicle-to-grid shown net, negative when selling back) with the smart-case price on the right axis. Uncontrolled charging lands on the evening peak; smart charging waits for the night trough; V2G discharges into the peak.
Fleet state of charge
Uncontrolled charging keeps the cars full; smart charging rides closer to the 20 % floor and fills up in the cheap hours; V2G cycles the batteries harder.
System cost and the fleet's electricity bill
Both fall from uncontrolled to smart to V2G: the fleet buys cheaper hours and, with V2G, earns the peak spread back.
