Chained hydro reservoirs: turbines, spill and water values

Water is counted in the energy it can make at a turbine. What the upper turbine releases arrives in the lower reservoir; what the upper spillway lets through arrives too, without making power. The price of water on each reservoir bus is the water value: what one more stored unit would save downstream.

One river, two power stations
Water over the year, in the energy a turbine can make from it (GWh). The upper reservoir's water leaves by its turbine or its spillway and lands in the lower reservoir either way, so water that goes through the upper turbine can make power there and again below: 248 and 315 GWh from the two turbines. Spilled water makes none, and a lot of the melt is spilled because the turbines are too small for it.
Reservoir levels
The large upper reservoir fills through the snowmelt fortnight and is drawn down in the dry one; the small lower reservoir cycles daily on what the upper turbine passes down. The gap in the middle is the unmodelled summer between the two fortnights.
Turbine and spill flows
Spill appears only when the lower reservoir is full and the turbines are at their limit during the melt; in the dry fortnight every unit of water goes through a turbine.
Electricity price and water values
The water bus prices are the marginal values of stored water. In the wet fortnight water is nearly free (spill is the alternative); in the dry one it rises to the thermal cost it displaces, and the lower reservoir's water is worth less than the upper's because it can only pass one turbine.
