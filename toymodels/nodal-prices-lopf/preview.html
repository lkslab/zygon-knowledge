Nodal prices in a congested triangle

Linear optimal power flow: Kirchhoff's voltage law ties the flows on the three lines of the triangle together, so when the cheap bus cannot export any more through the small line, the price splits by bus. The load is scaled through six levels to show the prices converge at low load and diverge once the line binds.

Power takes every path: one full line splits the prices
The three buses at peak load as a made-up country. Cheap coal in the north could serve the whole south, but the small A-B line is full. Power splits over every path, so more coal sent south, even towards C, would push part of it onto that line too; B and C must also run their dearer plants, and each bus gets its own price: cheap where power is stuck, dear where it is short.
Nodal prices by load level
One price while the network is unconstrained; once line A-B hits its limit the cheap bus A decouples and B and C pay their own marginal units.
Line flows at peak load
Absolute flow against thermal limit; A-B is at its limit, the loop path A-C carries the rest.
Congestion rent by line
Flow times the price difference across the line, the merchandising surplus a transmission owner collects; zero until a constraint binds. B-C goes negative: Kirchhoff's voltage law forces flow against the price gradient on the loop path.
