Price duration and cost recovery

Reuses the single-node capacity expansion at a 20 % CO2 cap. In a linear programme every technology built at the optimum recovers exactly its annualised capital plus operating cost from the market price: the zero-profit condition. The price duration curve shows where that revenue comes from, a few scarcity hours and long stretches at the marginal cost of gas or at zero.

Price duration curve (log scale)
A handful of scarcity hours at the value of lost load, a block at the gas marginal cost plus its CO2 shadow price, and a long tail at zero when wind and solar are surplus. Prices below 1 EUR/MWh are cut off by the log axis.
Revenue against annualised cost
Revenue beside the stacked cost (capital, fuel, and for gas the CO2 shadow price on its emissions). Every technology the optimiser built recovers its cost exactly: the zero-profit condition of a linear programme. Storage and hydrogen earn on price spreads.
Where the margin comes from
Margin above marginal cost (gas net of carbon) split into scarcity hours, when the price is above the gas level and set by storage or lost load, and ordinary hours.
