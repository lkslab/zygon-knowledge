Zero-price hours and wind revenue risk

The single-node capacity expansion is solved at a sweep of CO2 caps, so the optimiser builds more wind and solar at every step. Each case's hourly price is then read from the wind farm's side: how often the price sits at zero, what wind earns per MWh against the average price (its capture rate), and how much that earning swings from week to week. The zero-price hours are the ones a CfD or a PPA tops up.

More wind and solar: more zero-price hours, less per MWh for wind
Each point is one optimal system under a tighter CO2 cap. The share of hours at zero price rises, and what wind earns per MWh falls against the average price (capture rate), because wind produces most exactly when the price is zero.
What wind earns per MWh, week by week
Market revenue over output in each week, for the least and the most renewable case. With less wind and solar the week-to-week earning is steadier; with a lot it swings between zero in windy weeks and very high values in still ones. A CfD or a PPA replaces this line with a flat one.
Price duration curves by wind and solar share
The year's prices sorted from high to low (axis clipped at 500 EUR/MWh; scarcity hours go higher). The block at zero widens as wind and solar grow, and the remaining revenue concentrates in fewer, higher-priced hours.
