Sector coupling: electricity, heat and hydrogen

The single-node capacity expansion gains a heat bus (heat pump with a seasonal COP, gas boiler, hot-water tank, winter-peaked heat demand) and a hydrogen bus (electrolyser, store, fuel cell, a flat industrial hydrogen demand) under a tight CO2 cap. Coupling adds flexible electric load: the heat pump and electrolyser run when wind and solar are surplus, and the tank and hydrogen store carry that energy across hours and weeks.

Electricity balance, weekly means
Supply above zero, withdrawals below. The heat pump's draw peaks in winter, the electrolyser soaks up summer solar; the fuel cell returns a little of it in the dark weeks.
Heat balance, weekly means
At this cap the CO2 shadow price prices the gas boiler out entirely, so the heat pump carries all heat and the cheap tank absorbs surplus electricity as heat (its standing losses are why heat supplied exceeds heat demand). Loosen the cap knob to see the boiler return.
Flexible load in a winter week
Electric draw of the heat pump and the electrolyser against the electricity price: the electrolyser stops when the price rises, the heat pump keeps going because the tank is small.
