Spatial clustering of a six-bus network

Six buses in two meshed regions, a windy west and a load-heavy east, joined by two tie lines. The full network is solved with Kirchhoff's laws over one week, then clustered to two buses (one per region) with `n.cluster.spatial.cluster_by_busmap` and solved again. Internal congestion and the nodal price spread inside each region vanish; the tie-line capacity is summed. The comparison shows what a clustered model can and cannot say about prices, flows and cost.

Mean price per bus, nodal vs clustered
Clustering gives every bus in a region the region's price; the spread inside the west, where the wind buses are cheaper than the coal bus, is gone.
Mean line loading, nodal vs clustered
Internal lines do not exist after clustering; the two tie lines become one with their ratings summed, which lets the clustered model push more west-east flow than Kirchhoff's law allows on the real pair.
West-to-east transfer over the week
The clustered network transfers more in windy hours because the aggregated tie line ignores the loop-flow limit on the individual lines.
