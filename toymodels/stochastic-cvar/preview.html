Stochastic investment and CVaR risk aversion

One node, wind, solar, gas and a battery, two representative fortnights. The gas price is unknown when capacities are chosen: low, mid or high with given probabilities. Three deterministic plans (one per price), a two-stage stochastic plan that minimises expected cost, and CVaR-weighted plans that also penalise the expensive tail. The value of the stochastic solution and the expected value of perfect information fall out of comparing them.

Realised annual cost under each gas price
What each plan costs once the price is revealed. The CVaR plan pays a little more in the cheap scenarios to be cheaper in the expensive one.
Capacity by planning strategy
Each deterministic plan bets on one gas price. The stochastic plan hedges; the CVaR plan hedges harder, buying more wind and less gas to cap the expensive tail.
Expected vs worst-case cost as risk aversion grows
omega = 0 is the risk-neutral plan; omega = 1 minimises the tail alone. Expected cost rises monotonically, the worst case falls: the price of insurance.
