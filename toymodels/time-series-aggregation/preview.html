What averaging throws away
A September week of load minus the wind and solar the full-resolution plan builds: below zero there is power to spare, above zero something else must supply. Every 4 hours it swings by about 173 MW a day, surplus at midday and shortfall at night; averaged over 12 hours the swing shrinks to about 66 MW, and the battery that would move the midday surplus into the night looks useless.
Optimal capacity by aggregation method
Averaging over 12 hours flattens the daily solar peak and the battery disappears; representative fortnights keep the diurnal shape but miss the year's extremes, so the hydrogen store and the firm capacity move.
Annual system cost by aggregation method
The total cost of each method against the full resolution: averaging comes out low, because it smooths away the hard hours; the representative fortnights come out high, by an amount that depends on which weeks they include.
Price duration curves by aggregation method
Axis clipped at 400 EUR/MWh. The coarse methods lose the scarcity spike and the zero-price tail alike; both matter for anything that earns on price spreads.
