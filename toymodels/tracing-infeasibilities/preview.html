Tracing an infeasible dispatch problem

One bus, three days, two thermal units with ramp limits and minimum outputs, no load shedding, and a load spike that is both too high and too fast. The solve reports "infeasible" and nothing else. HiGHS can compute an irreducible infeasible subset (IIS): the smallest set of constraints and bounds that cannot hold together, read here back into PyPSA's constraint names and snapshots. Adding load shedding repairs the problem and the price then points at the same hours.

The load spike against what the fleet can reach
The spike exceeds the installed capacity and arrives faster than the ramp limits allow, so no dispatch satisfies every constraint. After the repair the served load is what the fleet can physically deliver.
Constraint families in the irreducible infeasible subset
The smallest set of constraints that cannot hold together, by PyPSA constraint name. Nodal balances at the spike hours together with the ramp and capacity limits that keep the units from meeting them.
Price after adding load shedding
With a slack at the value of lost load the problem solves, and the price reaches that value exactly in the hours the IIS pointed at, plus the ramp-constrained hours around them.
